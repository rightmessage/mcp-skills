# Questions

Ask one per turn, in plain text, in your reply. Skip any the data or the site already answered. Rephrase each around what you saw; the wording here is a starting point. When the user is stuck, offer one or two likely answers to react to.

## Ask every business (when not already answered)

1. **What's happening now.** "Is anything launching, on sale or ending in the next few weeks?" A deadline changes what comes first.
2. **The result and its value.** "What do you count each month: booked calls, orders, applications, signups? Roughly what is one worth?" Needed for projected impact. If they won't give a value, project in that result only.
3. **Where the result happens.** Ask when it isn't on a page RightMessage sees: "When someone buys, where do they finish? Is there a thank-you page on your site, or does it all happen on [checkout domain]?"
4. **Who changes things.** "Who can change the pages and the RightMessage setup?" Use the publish history to name who's active: "I see [name] has been publishing lately; is that still right?"
5. **What's been tried.** "Have you changed anything on the site for different visitors before? If you could change one thing for one group, what would it be?" Most people haven't, and don't know. That's what the recommendations are for.

## By business type (see router.md)

- **Sales-led service:** "After someone books, how many turn up, and how many become clients?" "Does anyone follow up people who start booking but don't pick a time?"
- **Online store:** "What's your average order, and how many orders are first orders?" "Do customers buy again, or subscribe?"
- **Self-serve software or product:** "Which kinds of customer stick around best?" "Do they each need the product explained differently?"
- **Application or program:** "When is the next cohort or launch?" "Of the applications you get, how many are a good fit?"
- **Course, membership or digital products:** "Which product do most new subscribers buy first?" "Does anything in your emails change based on what people told you?"
- **Publisher or content site:** "Can you say who sees which pop-up?" "What do you most want a reader to do next?"
- **Low-price first offer:** "How many who take the first offer go on to the upsell?"

## Only when they apply

- **Paid traffic:** "Most of your paid clicks land on [page]. Who runs those ads, and who can change that page?" "How many different ads or audiences point at [page]? Do you keep a copy of the page per ad, or one page for all of them?"
- **Lots of pop-ups:** "You have [N] pop-ups running. Can you say who sees which one?"
- **Answers collected:** "You've collected [N] answers to '[question]'. Does anything change for those people afterwards, in email or on the site?"
- **Broken or odd configuration:** "Two campaigns change [page] at once. Was that on purpose?" "[Flow] stopped running on [date]. Did the page change?"
- **Something stale:** "[Page or offer] still says [date or offer]. Is that still running?"
- **A second side of the business:** "You also sell to [organisations]. Should I look at that side, or stay with [main side]?"

## Dwell, then move on

If an answer is vague and would change the recommendation, ask once more, more narrowly: "Is that closer to 5 or 50?" Then move on with what you have and record it as an estimate. If the user doesn't know, lower your confidence in the ideas that depend on it.
