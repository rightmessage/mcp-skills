# Projected impact

Projected impact turns each idea into a number the user can weigh: extra leads, orders, applications or bookings, and their value, per month. It is a scenario built on the account's own numbers. It is never a promise or a benchmark.

## Formula

```
eligible visitors per month   (who would see the change, from the account)
× current conversion rate     (to the result that matters, see "Which rate" below)
× assumed lift (low to high)  (stated, see below)
= extra results per month

extra results × value per result        (the user's figure, or a labelled public price)
= extra revenue per month

extra results × current cost per result (from the ads report, when ads drive the page)
= ad spend it would take to buy the same results
```

The ad-spend line needs no figures from the user, and paid teams already think in it: "about $1,100 of ad spend a month you wouldn't need" (hypothetical example) is easier to weigh than a lift percentage.

Calculate with code, not in your head.

## Inputs

- **Eligible visitors:** only the visitors who would actually see the change, such as paid arrivals on that page, or matched visitors for that audience. Not whole-site traffic. Use the last 30 days, or sampled days scaled up and labelled when a tool refuses the range.
- **Same population:** the eligible visitors and the conversion rate must describe the same people over the same window. Don't divide one tool's conversions by another tool's visitors.
- **Value:** the user's figure first. If they don't know, a public price from the site can stand in, labelled "at list price" and treated as an upper bound for revenue. Without either, stop at results, plus the ad-spend line when there is one.
- **Site share:** site changes can only move results that come through the site. Use the measured on-site result (the goal's count) as the baseline, not the user's total, when the user says part of the total comes from outreach, referrals or booking links sent directly. Say how large the off-site share is, and when the user names a target for the total, say plainly how much of the gap site ideas could close and how much has to come from elsewhere.

## Which rate

Most accounts don't measure their money step yet, often because it happens on another domain. Use the first of these you have, and say which:

1. **The measured result** on that page (a confirmed booking, order or application goal).
2. **The nearest measured step,** named plainly: "reached checkout", "reached the application page", "clicked Apply". Project in that step, not in sales.
3. **A per-point scenario** when no step is measured: "every 1 point of conversion rate on this page is worth about N orders a month at this traffic; at a 5–15% lift that's N × r × 0.05 to N × r × 0.15 for your rate r."
4. **A borrowed rate** from a similar page in the same account, labelled as borrowed.

Don't invent an industry benchmark. Whatever you used, the measurement idea for that result belongs in the recommendations.

## Assumed lift

Use a range and say where it came from:
- If the account has a holdback result for a similar change, use its observed range, and say how many conversions it rests on.
- Otherwise use a deliberately modest range of **5% to 15%** for a change on an existing page, and say it's an assumption, not a result anyone has measured for them.
- For a new step that creates a path where none existed (a booking prompt at the end of an article, a product suggestion on the post-signup page), project from the visitors who'd see it and an assumed click or take-up rate, stated plainly.
- For fixing something broken (a dead form, a survey that stopped running), project from what it did before it broke, if the account shows it.
- For follow-up on people who started and didn't finish (submitted a form but never confirmed), project from that count: people a month who stopped × an assumed share recovered (say 10% to 20%), stated as an assumption.
- Measurement fixes and cleanup without a direct cost get "makes the others measurable" or "lowers risk" instead of a number.

## Confidence

- **High:** traffic and the conversion rate come from the account, with enough conversions to be stable, and the user confirmed the value.
- **Medium:** the account supplies traffic, but the rate is small, an intermediate step, or estimated.
- **Low:** most inputs are assumptions (click rates, borrowed rates, sampled days).

## Ranking with projected impact

- Compare ideas in the same result (orders with orders). When results differ (signups versus applications), rank by value if you have one; otherwise rank within each result and say so.
- A low-confidence idea with a large number doesn't beat a high-confidence idea with a modest one by default. Say why you ordered them.
- Never add the ideas' impacts together as if they were independent, round a scenario up into a headline, or present the high end alone.

## How to say it

Hypothetical example:

> About 1,900 paid visitors a month land on /pricing. 1.2% of them book a call today. If matching the page to each ad lifts that by 5% to 15%, that's about 1 to 3 more booked calls a month. At your 1 in 3 close rate and roughly $5,000 a client, about $1,900 to $5,700 a month. This is a scenario on your numbers, not a forecast.
